Social Democracy

The idea that markets are useful tools but poor masters — a capitalist economy, steered by a democratic welfare state, with civil liberties and international cooperation taken seriously.

Where Social Democracy sits
On the four dimensions
Economycenter-left
Civil Libertiesliberty-leaning
Societyprogressive
Global Outlookglobalist

Core beliefs

Universal healthcare, education, and a strong social safety net
Markets kept competitive — regulated where they fail, freed where they work
Strong unions and collective bargaining as a democratic counterweight
Liberal civil rights, free speech, and the rule of law
International cooperation on climate, trade, and human rights
Gradual, democratic reform rather than revolution

Key figures

Olof Palme · Sweden, 1927–1986

Prime minister who turned Sweden into the model welfare state and a voice for global solidarity.

Clement Attlee · United Kingdom, 1883–1967

Built the National Health Service and the post-war British welfare settlement.

Willy Brandt · Germany, 1913–1992

Chancellor of West Germany; pursued Ostpolitik and championed North–South justice.

Neighbours on the spectrum

Democratic Socialism
Further left

Wants ownership itself to change — worker control of industry, not just regulation of it.
Progressivism
Neighbor

Shares the goals, focuses more on reforming institutions and culture than on the economy.
Social Liberalism
Further right

Agrees on rights and reform, but puts more faith in markets and less in the state.

Social Democracy — questions, answered

What is social democracy in simple terms?

Keep the market economy, but make sure democracy — not money — sets its rules: universal public services, strong worker rights, and a safety net that no one can fall through.
