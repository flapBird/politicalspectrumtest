Liberalism

Liberalism starts from the individual: rights that precede the state, consent as the basis of government, and markets and tolerance as the default. Its classical wing wants the state small; its social wing wants the state to guarantee real opportunity.

Core beliefs

Individual rights — speech, conscience, association — that the state may not override
Government by consent and the rule of law, under a constitution
Free markets and free trade as the default economic arrangement
Tolerance of different lifestyles and beliefs
Secular public institutions
Skepticism of concentrated power — in states and monopolies alike

Key figures

John Locke · England, 1632–1704

Argued that life, liberty, and property are natural rights and that governments exist only by consent.

Adam Smith · Scotland, 1723–1790

Showed how self-interest in open markets can produce general prosperity.

John Stuart Mill · Britain, 1806–1873

Drew the line of liberty: the only justified limit on freedom is harm to others.

Liberalism — questions, answered

What is liberalism in simple terms?

The individual comes first. People have rights the state can’t take away, governments need consent, and markets and tolerance are the defaults. Disputes inside liberalism are mostly about how big the state should be to make those rights real.

What’s the difference between classical and social liberalism?

Classical liberalism wants a small “night-watchman” state: law, order, defense, and little else. Social liberalism argues that rights mean little without education, healthcare, and a floor under poverty — so the state should actively secure opportunity. Most center-left and center-right parties borrow from both.

Is liberalism left or right?

Both, historically: liberals led revolutions for constitutions and free markets, and today “liberal” means center-right in much of Europe and center-left in the United States. On our dimensions, liberalism is liberty-first on civil liberties and typically center-right on economics.

How is liberalism different from conservatism?

Liberals celebrate change and individual choice; conservatives see institutions and customs as accumulated wisdom that change should not rush. They can ally on markets and the rule of law, and split on social issues and the pace of reform.

How is liberalism different from libertarianism?

Libertarianism is classical liberalism taken further: taxes and regulation are treated as near-injustices and the state shrinks toward a minimum. Liberals generally accept taxation for public goods and some correction of markets.

Which parties are liberal?

Many carry the label: the UK Liberal Democrats, Germany’s FDP, the Dutch VVD, Canada’s Liberal Party — though the last governs well left of classical liberalism. In the US, “liberal” usually means the social-liberal center-left.

Where does liberalism come from?

From the 17th–18th century Enlightenment — Locke, Montesquieu, Smith — and the American and French revolutions. It supplied the vocabulary of constitutional government, rights, and markets that nearly every modern democracy now speaks.

What are the main criticisms of liberalism?

That it underestimates unequal starting points (the social-liberal reply), that markets produce concentrations of power liberalism itself distrusts, and that radical individualism can erode the communities individuals rely on.
