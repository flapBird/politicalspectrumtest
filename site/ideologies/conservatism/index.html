Conservatism

Conservatism treats society as an inheritance: customs, family, faith, and nation hold accumulated wisdom no blueprint can replace. Reform is welcome — when it respects what already works and moves at a pace people can absorb.

Key figures

Edmund Burke · Ireland and Britain, 1729–1797

Defended gradual reform against revolutionary abstraction; the intellectual father of modern conservatism.

Margaret Thatcher · United Kingdom, 1925–2013

Remade British conservatism around free markets, individual responsibility, and a strong state at home and abroad.

Angela Merkel · Germany, born 1954

Showed a pragmatic, cautious center-right — incremental change, stability, and coalition management.

Conservatism — questions, answered

What is conservatism in simple terms?

Don’t break what works. Society’s customs and institutions carry hard-won wisdom, so change should be careful, tested, and gradual — and some things (family, faith, nation) deserve protection regardless of fashion.

Is conservatism left or right?

Usually center-right: right on economics (markets, lower taxes) and traditional on social issues. But conservatism is defined more by its attitude to change than by any fixed policy — one-nation conservatives have backed welfare states enthusiastically.

What is “one-nation” conservatism?

A British tradition, from Benjamin Disraeli onward, holding that society is one body and the better-off have obligations to the worse-off. It accepts a welfare state and public services to keep the nation whole.

How is conservatism different from libertarianism?

Both favor markets, but conservatism accepts state power for order, borders, and tradition, while libertarianism wants the state out of both the economy and private life. Conservatives also keep institutions — the army, the church, the family — that libertarians view with suspicion.

Do conservatives oppose all change?

No — Burke’s point was that a state without the ability to reform cannot preserve anything. Conservatism prefers incremental, evidence-tested reform to transformation; its quarrel is with pace and hubris, not direction as such.

Which parties are conservative?

The UK Conservatives, Germany’s CDU/CSU, Japan’s LDP, India’s BJP, and the US Republican Party all sit in the conservative family, though each blends conservatism differently with liberalism or nationalism.

What role does religion play in conservatism?

Historically central — conservatism defended established churches and religious morality. Today most conservative parties govern secularly but still defend faith communities’ place in public life.

What are the main criticisms of conservatism?

That “institutions carry wisdom” can be cover for entrenched privilege, that gradualism just delays necessary reform, and that defending tradition can slide into defending hierarchy. Conservatives answer that reformers’ certainty has a worse track record.
