Libertarianism

Libertarianism starts from self-ownership: your life, labor, and property are yours. The state’s job shrinks to protecting people from force and fraud — everything else, from schools to sobriety, is left to voluntary choice and free markets.

Core beliefs

Self-ownership: your body, labor, and property are yours by right
The non-aggression principle: force is justified only in defense
A minimal state — courts, police, defense — or none at all
Free markets, free trade, and stable money
Legal personal choices: speech, association, drugs, arms (extent debated)
Skepticism of bureaucracy, surveillance, and foreign wars

Key figures

John Stuart Mill · Britain, 1806–1873

The harm principle — liberty needs no justification; limiting it does.

Friedrich Hayek · Austria and Britain, 1899–1992

Argued central planning fails because knowledge is dispersed; markets aggregate it.

Milton Friedman · United States, 1912–2006

Made the case for free markets, school choice, and voluntary solutions in accessible prose.

Libertarianism — questions, answered

What is libertarianism in simple terms?

Live and let live, and keep the state small. People should be free to run their lives, spend their money, and make their own deals; government exists only to stop force, theft, and fraud.

What’s the difference between left- and right-libertarianism?

Both want maximal liberty and a minimal state; they differ on property. Right-libertarians treat private property as foundational; left-libertarians accept strong personal liberty but question how property holdings became unequal. In the US, “libertarian” usually means the right-libertarian version.

How is libertarianism different from liberalism?

Liberalism accepts taxation for public goods, antitrust, and a social floor; libertarianism treats most taxation as near-confiscation and most regulation as market interference. Classical liberalism is its more compromise-ready cousin.

What do libertarians think about taxes?

Most see them as justified only for core functions — courts, police, defense — or not at all; anarcho-capitalists would replace taxation with voluntary payment. Mainstream libertarians often favor flat or consumption taxes.

What about drugs, guns, and speech?

Consistently permissive: legalize drugs, protect gun rights, and defend nearly all speech — positions that follow from self-ownership and the non-aggression principle. Specific edges, like age limits and weapon types, are debated internally.

Which parties are libertarian?

The US Libertarian Party is the flagship, with smaller counterparts elsewhere. In other countries, classical-liberal parties — Germany’s FDP, Switzerland’s FDP — carry parts of the agenda.

Where does libertarianism come from?

From radical currents of classical liberalism (Auberon Herbert, Lysander Spooner) and from 20th-century economists Hayek, Friedman, and Mises, plus Ayn Rand’s popular fiction. The US Libertarian Party launched in 1971.

What are the main criticisms of libertarianism?

That markets need referees and public goods — roads, research, clean air — that markets under-supply; that “freedom of contract” can mask coercion by poverty; and that a minimal state can’t deliver the security markets presuppose.
