National Conservatism

National conservatism puts the nation-state at the center of political life: sovereignty over integration, borders over openness, and inherited culture over cosmopolitan pluralism. It accepts a market economy — but reserves the right to steer it for national ends.

Core beliefs

National sovereignty and self-government above international bodies
Controlled borders and selective immigration
The nation as a community of shared history, culture, and often faith
Traditional family and social norms in public life
Law and order and a strong national defense
A market economy the nation may steer — industrial policy, strategic sectors

Key figures

The model of sovereignty-first politics: strong state, independent France, a “Europe of nations”.

National Conservatism — questions, answered

What is national conservatism in simple terms?

The nation comes first: its borders, its laws, its traditions. National conservatives want decisions made domestically rather than by international bodies, controlled immigration, and public life anchored in inherited culture.

How is it different from ordinary conservatism?

It’s conservatism with sovereignty and cultural identity promoted to first principles. Ordinary conservatism manages change prudently; national conservatism mobilizes for cultural and constitutional conflict — with the EU, migration, or “woke” institutions.

How is it different from populism?

They overlap heavily but differ in structure: populism is a people-versus-elite frame that can be left or right; national conservatism is a fuller program — sovereignty, borders, tradition — held by parties that aim to govern durably.

What do national conservatives think about the economy?

They accept markets but subordinate them to the national interest: industrial policy, protection of strategic sectors, and skepticism of global free-trade maximalism. Welfare, they often say, should first serve citizens.

Why the hostility to the EU and global institutions?

Because pooled sovereignty is exactly what they oppose. From their view, treaties and courts that override national parliaments weaken self-government; their answer is cooperation by agreement, not integration.

Is national conservatism new?

The ingredients are old — patriotism, tradition, sovereignty — but the label and its current form date to the 2010s, crystallizing around Brexit, Orbán’s Hungary, and the NatCon conference series.

Which parties are national conservative?

Hungary’s Fidesz, Italy’s Brothers of Italy, the pro-Brexit British right, France’s Rassemblement National, and parts of the US Republican right. Labels vary: “national right”, “sovereigntist”, “new right”.

What are the main criticisms of national conservatism?

That it shades from patriotism into exclusion of minorities, that border control alone can’t manage migration or climate, and that appeals to “sovereignty” can excuse democratic backsliding — critics point to Hungary. Supporters reply that self-government and cultural cohesion are preconditions of any healthy democracy.
