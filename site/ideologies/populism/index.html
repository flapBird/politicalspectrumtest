Populism

Populism is a frame, not a fixed platform: society is split into “the people” and “the corrupt elite”, and politics should return power to the former. It can lean left (against bankers and corporations) or right (against cultural elites and global institutions) — what’s constant is the confrontation.

Key figures

Populism — questions, answered

What is populism in simple terms?

Politics as “the people versus the elite”. Populists claim ordinary people share a common interest that a corrupt establishment — parties, courts, media, experts — ignores, and they promise to give power back.

Is populism left or right?

Both. Left-populism targets bankers, corporations, and oligarchs (Chávez, Spain’s Podemos, Sanders-style rhetoric); right-populism targets cultural elites, immigration, and global institutions (Trump, France’s RN). The style is shared; the villains differ.

Is populism democratic — or a threat to democracy?

Both readings exist, and populists straddle them. It’s democratic in claiming to restore popular sovereignty and turnout; it’s destabilizing when “the people” means only supporters, and courts, press, and minorities become enemies. The record ranges from renewed participation to democratic breakdown.

Why is populism rising?

Stagnant wages, austerity, migration shocks, and the 2008 financial crisis eroded trust in mainstream parties; social media rewards confrontation over coalition. Populism supplies blame, identity, and simplicity.

How is populism different from fascism?

Fascism adds a cult of violence, one-party dictatorship, and the suppression of all pluralism. Populism can stay electoral; fascism by definition cannot. Overlaps appear when populists dismantle checks — which is why scholars watch the trajectory, not just the label.

Which parties are populist?

Argentina’s Peronists, Italy’s Five Star Movement, France’s Rassemblement National, the US MAGA current, Spain’s Podemos, Hungary’s Fidesz (nationalist-populist) — and dozens more. It is a global political style.

What do populists actually do in office?

Deliver visible benefits fast (cash transfers, pensions), attack intermediaries (courts, media, central banks), centralize power, and lean on referendums. Performance depends heavily on institutions and available resources.

What are the main criticisms of populism?

That “the people” is a fiction covering majoritarianism, that scapegoating corrodes minority rights, and that simple promises meet complex problems. Populists answer that establishment politics ignored real grievances for decades.
